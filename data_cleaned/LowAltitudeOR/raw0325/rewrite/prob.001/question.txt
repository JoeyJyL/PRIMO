A UAV-based last-mile delivery company, SkyDrone Logistics, is planning the deployment of an unmanned aerial vehicle distribution network across a dense urban region that features a significant restricted airspace (obstacle).
The firm seeks to strategically establish a small number of energy replenishment relay depots so as to maximize the total weighted demand of reachable service points.
Because UAVs traverse point-to-point along straight-line trajectories rather than road networks, any blocked corridor imposed by the no-fly zone forces detour routing; thus, the relevant distance measure is the Euclidean Shortest Path (ESP).

**Operational Rules:**
1. **Relay Network Connectivity:** Drones transport parcels originating from the Central Depot (Warehouse) through a chain of Relay Depots. Every active depot must maintain a connected path back to the Warehouse such that the ESP distance between any two successive stops along that path does not exceed the Max-Payload Range ($f_p$).
2. **Service Point Reachability:** A demand node (customer) is deemed "covered" when at least one operational depot (or the Warehouse itself) lies within the Delivery Range ($f_d$) of that node—guaranteeing the UAV can complete a round-trip package drop.
3. **Infrastructure Budget:** Counting the pre-existing Warehouse, the total number of operational facilities must not surpass $p$.

- Delivery Range ($f_d$): 6.0 km
- Max Stations ($p$): 3 (1 Warehouse + 2 New Stations)
