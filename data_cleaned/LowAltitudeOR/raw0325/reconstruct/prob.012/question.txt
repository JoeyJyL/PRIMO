MedFlight EMS is designing a drone-based emergency network to deliver naloxone for opioid overdose response.
**Goal:** Determine the optimal locations of drone bases (DBs), number of drones at each base, and drone dispatching policy to **minimize the average response time** across all overdose requests.

**Problem Setting:**
- Overdose-triggered requests (OTRs) occur at various locations following a Poisson process.
- Each drone base operates as an M/G/K queueing system where K (number of drones) is a decision variable.
- Response time = Queueing delay (waiting for available drone) + Flight time (drone travel to patient).
- Drones have limited coverage radius due to battery constraints.
- The system must be stable: arrival rate < service rate at each base.

**Network Design Decisions:**
1. Which candidate locations to open as drone bases?
2. How many drones to deploy at each opened base (1 or 2)?
3. Which OTR locations to assign to which drone base?

**Queueing Model:**
- Arrival rate at base j: $\eta_j = \sum_{i \in I_j} \lambda_i \cdot y_{ij}$
- Expected service time for OTR at i served by base j: $E[S_{ij}] = \frac{d_{ij}}{v} + E[\xi]$
- Queueing delay approximation (M/G/K): depends on arrival rate, service time, and number of drones K

**Parameters:**
- Total drones available: p = 5
- Maximum drone bases to open: q = 4
- Maximum drones per base: M = 2
- Drone speed: v = 30 units/hour
- Drone coverage radius: r = 25 units
- Expected non-travel service time: E[ξ] = 0.4 hours (includes naloxone administration, drone reset)

**Stability Constraint:**
- At each base j: $\sum_{i} \lambda_i \cdot y_{ij} < \frac{K_j}{\sum_{i} \lambda_i \cdot y_{ij} \cdot E[S_{ij}]}$

**Objective:**
Minimize the average response time across all OTRs, accounting for both queueing delays and flight times.
