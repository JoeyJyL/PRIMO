## Sets and Indices

- $K = \{1, 3, 4, 5\}$: candidate vertiport zones
- $W = \{1\}$: warehouses
- $C = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$: customer demand zones
- $T$: vertiport types (small, medium, large)
- $T_k \subseteq T$: available vertiport types for zone $k$ (based on infrastructure)

## Parameters

- $w_c$: demand weight (packages) of customer zone $c$
- $S_k$: safety score of zone $k$ (higher = worse)
- $N_k$: noise score of zone $k$ (higher = worse)
- $\Gamma_t$: capacity (packages/day) of vertiport type $t$
- $v_{wck} \in \{0,1\}$: 1 if warehouse $w$ can reach customer $c$ via vertiport $k$ (within drone range $f_p = 30$ km and last-mile distance $f_d = 10$ km)
- $d_{\min} = 2$ km, $d_{\max} = 10$ km: safety distance bounds
- $p = 3$: number of vertiports to select

## Decision Variables

- $x_{kt} \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 if vertiport of type $t$ is placed in zone $k$
- $y_c \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 if customer zone $c$ is covered
- $z_{ck} \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 if customer $c$ is exclusively assigned to vertiport $k$

## Objective

Weighted-sum multi-objective (minimize):

$$\min \; 1000 \sum_{c \in C} w_c (1 - y_c) + 10 \sum_{k \in K} \sum_{t \in T_k} S_k \cdot x_{kt} + 10 \sum_{k \in K} \sum_{t \in T_k} N_k \cdot x_{kt}$$

## Constraints

### (C1) Vertiport Budget

$$\sum_{k \in K} \sum_{t \in T_k} x_{kt} = p$$

### (C2) Single Type per Zone

$$\sum_{t \in T_k} x_{kt} \le 1, \quad \forall k \in K$$

### (C3) Coverage Definition

$$y_c \le \sum_{k \in K} \sum_{t \in T_k} x_{kt} \cdot v_{wck}, \quad \forall c \in C, w \in W$$

### (C4) Exclusive Assignment — Binary Assignment Formulation

The original formulation uses continuous fractional allocation variables $r_{wck} \in [0,1]$ to route demand fractions through vertiports, with the constraint $\sum_k r_{wck} = y_c$ and capacity limits on fractional demand. This allows demand splitting across multiple vertiports.

This is replaced by **binary exclusive assignment**: each covered customer is assigned to exactly one vertiport via binary variable $z_{ck} \in \{0,1\}$.

$$z_{ck} \le \sum_{t \in T_k} x_{kt} \cdot v_{wck}, \quad \forall c \in C, k \in K, w \in W$$

### (C5) Coverage Requirement — Exclusive Assignment

$$\sum_{k \in K} z_{ck} = y_c, \quad \forall c \in C$$

Each covered customer is assigned to exactly one vertiport (no demand splitting).

### (C6) Capacity Constraint — Binary Assignment

$$\sum_{c \in C} w_c \cdot z_{ck} \le \sum_{t \in T_k} \Gamma_t \cdot x_{kt}, \quad \forall k \in K$$

Total demand assigned to vertiport $k$ cannot exceed its type-dependent capacity.

### (C7) Minimum Safety Distance

$$x_{k_1 t_1} + x_{k_2 t_2} \le 1, \quad \forall k_1, k_2: k_1 \ne k_2, \; dist(k_1, k_2) < d_{\min}, \; \forall t_1 \in T_{k_1}, t_2 \in T_{k_2}$$

### (C8) Maximum Safety Distance

$$\sum_{\substack{k' \in K: k' \ne k \\ dist(k,k') \le d_{\max}}} \sum_{t' \in T_{k'}} x_{k't'} \ge \sum_{t \in T_k} x_{kt}, \quad \forall k \in K$$

## Key Modeling Difference

The original formulation uses **continuous fractional allocation variables** $r_{wck} \in [0,1]$ to represent demand fractions routed through each vertiport, allowing packages from a single customer zone to be split across multiple vertiports. The LP relaxation is tighter but allows fractional demand routing.

This reformulation uses **binary exclusive assignment variables** $z_{ck} \in \{0,1\}$, which forces each customer zone to be assigned to exactly one vertiport (whole-demand assignment). This eliminates fractional demand splitting and simplifies the allocation constraints from continuous to purely binary, making the model a pure MILP without continuous allocation variables.
