### Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

Let's define the decision variables for the problem.

Let's denote:

- $ y_j $: a binary variable indicating whether hub $ j $ is built (1) or not (0).
- $ x_{jis} $: a binary variable indicating whether demand point $ i $ is assigned to hub $ j $ in scenario $ s $ (1) or not (0).
- $ \phi_s $: a continuous auxiliary variable representing the total operational cost in scenario $ s $.
- $ Z $: a continuous auxiliary variable representing the worst-case operational cost across all scenarios.

Thus, we have:

- $ y_j \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ j \in H $.
- $ x_{jis} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ j \in H $, $ i \in C $, $ s \in \mathcal{S} $, where $ d(j, i) \le r $.
- $ \phi_s \ge 0 $ for all $ s \in \mathcal{S} $.
- $ Z \ge 0 $.

Where:
- $ H $ is the set of candidate hubs.
- $ C $ is the set of demand points.
- $ \mathcal{S} $ is the set of scenarios.
- $ r $ is the maximum service radius.

### Step 2: Objective Function Construction

The goal is to minimize the sum of construction cost and worst-case operational cost.

The objective function is:

$$
\text{Minimize } \sum_{j \in H} f_j y_j + Z
$$

Where:

- $ f_j $ is the construction cost of hub $ j $.

### Step 3: Constraint Construction

1. **Scenario Cost Definition (Auxiliary Variable):**

The auxiliary variable $ \phi_s $ captures the total operational cost in scenario $ s $.

For each scenario $ s \in \mathcal{S} $:

$$
\phi_s = \sum_{j \in H} \sum_{i \in C} q_{is} \cdot d_{ji} \cdot c_t \cdot x_{jis}
$$

Where:
- $ q_{is} $ is the demand of point $ i $ in scenario $ s $.
- $ d_{ji} $ is the distance from hub $ j $ to point $ i $.
- $ c_t $ is the unit transportation cost.

2. **Worst-Case Cost Definition (Min-Max Linearization):**

The auxiliary variable $ Z $ must be at least as large as every scenario's operational cost.

For each scenario $ s \in \mathcal{S} $:

$$
Z \ge \phi_s
$$

3. **Mandatory Coverage Constraint:**

In every scenario, every demand point must be assigned to exactly one active hub within range.

For each $ s \in \mathcal{S} $, $ i \in C $:

$$
\sum_{j \in H_i} x_{jis} = 1
$$

Where:
- $ H_i = \{j \in H : d(j, i) \le r\} $ is the set of hubs within service range of point $ i $.

4. **Big-M Aggregate Capacity Constraint:**

Instead of individual linking constraints $ x_{jis} \le y_j $, we use an aggregated Big-M capacity constraint. If hub $ j $ is not built, no demand point can be assigned to it across all points in any scenario.

For each $ j \in H $, $ s \in \mathcal{S} $:

$$
\sum_{i \in C_j} x_{jis} \le M_j \cdot y_j
$$

Where:
- $ C_j = \{i \in C : d(j, i) \le r\} $ is the set of demand points reachable from hub $ j $.
- $ M_j = |C_j| $ is the Big-M value, equal to the number of reachable demand points from hub $ j $.
