### Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

Let's define the decision variables for the problem.

- $ x_j \in \{0, 1\} $: binary variable indicating if vertiport $ j $ is opened, for all $ j \in J $.
- $ y_i \in \{0, 1\} $: binary variable indicating if demand zone $ i $ is covered, for all $ i \in I $.

### Step 2: Objective Function Construction

The objective maximizes the weighted score. We introduce a **composite penalty coefficient** $ P_j $ that merges construction cost and noise impact into a single per-vertiport penalty:

$$
P_j = W_{cost} \cdot C_j + W_{noise} \cdot N_j
$$

The objective function then simplifies to:

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = W_{cov} \sum_{i \in I} D_i \, y_i - \sum_{j \in J} P_j \, x_j
$$

Where:

- $ W_{cov} = 10 $, $ W_{cost} = 1 $, $ W_{noise} = 2 $.
- $ D_i $ is the demand of zone $ i $.
- $ C_j $ is the construction cost and $ N_j $ is the noise impact of vertiport $ j $.

### Step 3: Constraint Construction

1. **Budget (Count) Constraint:**

$$
\sum_{j \in J} x_j \le P
$$

2. **Coverage via OR General Constraint:**

Instead of the original aggregate coverage inequality $ y_i \le \sum_{j} a_{ij} x_j $, we use a **logical OR constraint** that directly models the coverage semantics: zone $ i $ is covered if and only if at least one vertiport capable of covering it is opened.

For each zone $ i \in I $:

$$
y_i = \bigvee_{j \in J:\, a_{ij}=1} x_j
$$

This is implemented as a solver-native **OR general constraint** (`addGenConstrOr`). Compared to the original inequality formulation:
- The original $ y_i \le \sum_j a_{ij} x_j $ only enforces $ y_i \le 1 $ when a covering station is open (upper bound), relying on maximization to push $ y_i $ up.
- The OR constraint enforces **equivalence**: $ y_i = 1 $ if and only if at least one covering station is open. This makes the model valid regardless of objective direction.

3. **Binary Constraints:**

$$
x_j, y_i \in \{0, 1\}
$$

Where $ a_{ij} = 1 $ if the Euclidean distance from zone $ i $ to vertiport $ j $ is at most $ R_{max} $.

### Summary of the Mathematical Model

**Decision Variables:**

- $ x_j \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ j \in J $.
- $ y_i \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i \in I $.

**Objective Function:**

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = W_{cov} \sum_{i \in I} D_i \, y_i - \sum_{j \in J} P_j \, x_j
$$

where $ P_j = W_{cost} \cdot C_j + W_{noise} \cdot N_j $.

**Constraints:**

$$
\sum_{j \in J} x_j \le P
$$

$$
y_i = \bigvee_{j:\, a_{ij}=1} x_j \quad \forall\, i \in I
$$

$$
x_j, y_i \in \{0, 1\}
$$
