### Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

**Sets:**
- $K$: Set of candidate vertiport zones, indexed by $k$.
- $I$: Set of warehouses, indexed by $i$.
- $J$: Set of destinations, indexed by $j$.

**Parameters:**
- $W_{ij}$: Demand (packages/day) from warehouse $i$ to destination $j$.
- $v_k^{ij} \in \{0, 1\}$: Coverage indicator; equals 1 iff $\text{dist}(i, k) \leq 30$ km and $\text{dist}(k, j) \leq 10$ km.
- $\Gamma$: Vertiport capacity (packages/day).
- $p$: Number of vertiports to open.
- $d_{\min}$: Minimum safety distance between vertiports.

**Decision Variables:**

- $z_k \in \{0, 1\}$: Binary variable equal to 1 if vertiport $k$ is opened; 0 otherwise.
- $f_k^{ij} \geq 0$: Continuous variable representing the actual package flow (packages/day) from warehouse $i$ to destination $j$ routed through vertiport $k$.

Note: The original formulation uses routing fraction $R_k^{ij} \in [0,1]$. Here we reformulate using actual flow $f_k^{ij} = W_{ij} \cdot R_k^{ij}$, which enables a natural Big-M linking where the demand value $W_{ij}$ serves as the Big-M coefficient.

### Step 2: Objective Function Construction

Maximize the total package demand served:

$$
\max Z = \sum_{k \in K} \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J} f_k^{ij}
$$

### Step 3: Constraint Construction

1. **Vertiport Count Constraint:**

$$
\sum_{k \in K} z_k = p
$$

2. **Big-M Coverage Linking Constraint:**

Flow through vertiport $k$ for OD pair $(i, j)$ is feasible only if $k$ is open and covers the pair. The Big-M coefficient is the demand $W_{ij}$:

$$
f_k^{ij} \leq W_{ij} \cdot v_k^{ij} \cdot z_k \quad \forall k \in K, i \in I, j \in J
$$

When $z_k = 0$: $f_k^{ij} \leq 0$, so no flow.
When $z_k = 1$ and $v_k^{ij} = 1$: $f_k^{ij} \leq W_{ij}$ (Big-M bound).
When $v_k^{ij} = 0$: $f_k^{ij} \leq 0$ regardless of $z_k$.

3. **Capacity Constraint:**

Total flow through vertiport $k$ cannot exceed its capacity:

$$
\sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J} f_k^{ij} \leq \Gamma \cdot z_k \quad \forall k \in K
$$

4. **Demand Conservation Constraint:**

Total flow for each OD pair across all vertiports cannot exceed the demand:

$$
\sum_{k \in K} f_k^{ij} \leq W_{ij} \quad \forall i \in I, j \in J
$$

5. **Safety Distance Constraint:**

Any two selected vertiports must be at least $d_{\min}$ apart:

$$
z_{k_1} + z_{k_2} \leq 1 \quad \forall k_1, k_2 \in K \text{ where } \text{dist}(k_1, k_2) < d_{\min}
$$

6. **Variable Bounds:**

$$
z_k \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall k \in K
$$
$$
f_k^{ij} \geq 0 \quad \forall k \in K, i \in I, j \in J
$$

### Summary of the Mathematical Model

**Decision Variables:**
- $z_k \in \{0, 1\}$: vertiport opening indicator.
- $f_k^{ij} \geq 0$: actual package flow through vertiport $k$ for OD pair $(i,j)$.

**Objective Function:**
$$
\max Z = \sum_{k \in K} \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J} f_k^{ij}
$$

**Constraints:**
1. Vertiport count: $\sum_k z_k = p$.
2. Big-M coverage linking: $f_k^{ij} \leq W_{ij} \cdot v_k^{ij} \cdot z_k$.
3. Capacity: $\sum_{i,j} f_k^{ij} \leq \Gamma \cdot z_k$.
4. Demand conservation: $\sum_k f_k^{ij} \leq W_{ij}$.
5. Safety distance: $z_{k_1} + z_{k_2} \leq 1$ for close pairs.
6. Variable bounds.

1. Optimization Problem Classification: MILP (Mixed-Integer Linear Program)
2. Problem Scale: Medium (8 candidates, 3 warehouses, 10 destinations, 240 continuous + 8 binary variables)
3. Solution Method: Exact MILP solver (Gurobi)
