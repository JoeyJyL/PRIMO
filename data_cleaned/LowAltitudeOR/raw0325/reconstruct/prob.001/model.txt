### Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

Let's define the decision variables for the problem.

Let's denote:

- $ X_j $: a binary variable indicating whether station site $ j $ is selected/active (1) or not (0).
- $ Y_k $: a binary variable indicating whether customer $ k $ is covered (1) or not (0).
- $ U_{ij} $: a binary variable indicating whether station $ i $ is the parent of station $ j $ in the connectivity tree rooted at the warehouse (defined only for pairs $ (i, j) $ with $ i \neq j $ and $ d(i, j) \le f_p $).
- $ L_i $: a continuous variable representing the level (hop distance from the warehouse) of station $ i $ in the connectivity tree.

Thus, we have:

- $ X_j \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ j \in S $.
- $ Y_k \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ k \in C $.
- $ U_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ (i, j) $ where $ i \neq j $, $ d(i, j) \le f_p $.
- $ L_i \ge 0 $ for all $ i \in S $.

### Step 2: Objective Function Construction

The goal is to maximize the total covered demand of the customers.

The objective function is:

$$
\text{Maximize } \sum_{k \in C} a_k Y_k
$$

Where:

- $ C $ is the set of customer nodes.
- $ a_k $ is the demand weight of customer $ k $.

### Step 3: Constraint Construction

Now, let's identify and translate the constraints into mathematical expressions.

1. **Coverage Definition Constraint:**

A customer $ k $ is covered only if at least one active station is within the allowable delivery range ($ N_k $).

For each customer $ k \in C $:

$$
\sum_{j \in N_k} X_j \ge Y_k
$$

Where:
- $ N_k $ is the set of candidate sites within distance $ f_d $ of customer $ k $.

2. **Station Budget Constraint:**

The total number of active stations (including the warehouse) must equal the budget $ p $.

$$
\sum_{i \in S} X_i = p
$$

Where:
- $ S $ is the set of all candidate station sites.
- $ p $ is the maximum number of stations to locate.

3. **Parent Assignment Constraint (MTZ Arborescence):**

Each active non-warehouse station must have exactly one parent station in the connectivity tree.

For all $ j \in S \setminus W $:

$$
\sum_{i \in \Omega_j} U_{ij} = X_j
$$

Where:
- $ \Omega_j $ is the set of candidate sites within distance $ f_p $ of station $ j $ (excluding $ j $ itself).
- $ W $ is the set of warehouse nodes.

4. **Parent Must Be Active Constraint:**

A parent arc can only exist if the parent station is active.

For all valid $ (i, j) $:

$$
U_{ij} \le X_i
$$

5. **MTZ Level Constraint:**

If station $ i $ is the parent of station $ j $, then the level of $ j $ must be at least one greater than the level of $ i $. This prevents cycles and ensures tree connectivity.

For all valid $ (i, j) $:

$$
L_j \ge L_i + 1 - M(1 - U_{ij})
$$

Where:
- $ M = |S| $ is a sufficiently large constant.

6. **Warehouse Level Constraint:**

The warehouse is the root of the tree and has level 0.

$$
L_0 = 0
$$

7. **Level Bound Constraint:**

The level of any station is bounded by the total number of stations minus one.

For all $ i \in S $:

$$
0 \le L_i \le |S| - 1
$$
