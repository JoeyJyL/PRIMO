This model is based on the Revenue Maximization (REV) variant of the Air Taxi Skyport Location Problem, which is formulated as a single-allocation p-hub median problem with choice-constrained elastic demand. 

## Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

Before defining the decision variables, we establish the fundamental sets and parameters governing the model:
* Let $\mathcal{L} = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ represent the set of origin zones, which also serve as candidate skyport locations.
* Let $\mathcal{J} = \{A, B\}$ represent the set of destination airports.
* Let $p = 2$ define the budget constraint for the number of skyports to open.

The decision variables for this model are defined as follows:
* Let $y_k$ denote a binary variable that equals 1 if a skyport is opened at candidate location $k \in \mathcal{L}$, and 0 otherwise.
* Let $x_{ikj}$ denote a binary variable that equals 1 if demand at origin $i \in \mathcal{L}$ for airport $j \in \mathcal{J}$ is routed via skyport $k \in \mathcal{L}$, and 0 otherwise.

## Step 2: Objective Function Construction

The objective function aims to maximize the total estimated air taxi revenue across all origin-skyport-airport combinations. Since the choice probabilities and fares are pre-computed constants, this objective remains linear in relation to the routing variable $x_{ikj}$.

$$Z=\sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}} \sum_{k \in \mathcal{L}} (f_{ik} + f_{kj}) \cdot \theta_{ikj} \cdot D_{ij} \cdot x_{ikj}$$

The parameters used in the objective formula are defined as:
* **$f_{ik} + f_{kj}$** represents the operator revenue per air taxi passenger, consisting of the access fare plus the flight fare, explicitly excluding the transfer cost.
* **$\theta_{ikj}$** denotes the pre-computed population choice probability of using an air taxi from origin $i$ to airport $j$ via skyport $k$.
* **$D_{ij}$** denotes the pre-computed monthly demand from origin $i$ to airport $j$.

## Step 3: Constraints Construction

Subject to the following constraints:

**1. Single Allocation Constraint**
This constraint ensures that each origin-airport pair must be assigned to exactly one skyport.
$$\sum_{k \in \mathcal{L}} x_{ikj} = 1 \quad \forall i \in \mathcal{L}, j \in \mathcal{J}$$
This guarantees that the total fractional assignments for any specific origin $i$ and destination $j$ sum to exactly 1 across all available skyports $k$ in set $\mathcal{L}$.

**2. Route Activation (Hub Linking) Constraint**
Equation (2) guarantees that demand can be routed via skyport $k$ only if skyport $k$ is open.
$$x_{ikj} \leq y_k \quad \forall i \in \mathcal{L}, k \in \mathcal{L}, j \in \mathcal{J}$$
This logically links the routing decision $x_{ikj}$ to the facility location variable $y_k$, ensuring passengers are not routed through unbuilt facilities.

**3. Budget Constraint**
This enforces the condition that exactly $p$ skyports must be opened.
$$\sum_{k \in \mathcal{L}} y_k = p$$
Here, $p$ represents the pre-defined limit on the total number of operational skyports.

**4. Mutual Exclusion Constraint**
This constraint ensures that skyports at locations 1 and 2 cannot both be opened due to airspace conflict.
$$y_1 + y_2 \leq 1$$
This restricts the binary facility location variables for candidate locations 1 and 2.

**5. Binary Constraints**
This constraint enforces the condition that the decision variables must strictly take on binary values.
$$y_k \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall k \in \mathcal{L}$$
$$x_{ikj} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall i \in \mathcal{L}, k \in \mathcal{L}, j \in \mathcal{J}$$

---

## Mathematical Model Summary

The complete mathematical formulation is summarized as follows:

$$\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}} \sum_{k \in \mathcal{L}} (f_{ik} + f_{kj}) \cdot \theta_{ikj} \cdot D_{ij} \cdot x_{ikj}$$

Subject to:
$$\sum_{k \in \mathcal{L}} x_{ikj} = 1 \quad \forall i, j$$
$$x_{ikj} \leq y_k \quad \forall i, k, j$$
$$\sum_{k \in \mathcal{L}} y_k = p$$
$$y_1 + y_2 \leq 1$$
$$y_k, x_{ikj} \in \{0, 1\}$$
