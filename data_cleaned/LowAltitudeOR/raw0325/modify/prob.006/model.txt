### Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

Let's define the decision variables for the problem.

Let's denote:

- $ x_{ij} $: a binary variable indicating if customer $ i $ is served by facility $ j $.
- $ y_j $: a binary variable indicating if facility $ j $ is opened.

Thus, we have:

- $ x_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i \in I $ and $ j \in J $.
- $ y_j \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ j \in J $.

### Step 2: Objective Function Construction

The goal is to maximize the total covered demand weighted by the importance of each customer.

The objective function is:

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{j \in J} \sum_{i \in I} w_i x_{ij}
$$

Where:

- $ w_i $ is the demand or weight of customer $ i $.
- $ I $ is the set of demand nodes (customers).
- $ J $ is the set of candidate facility locations.

### Step 3: Constraint Construction

Now, let's identify and translate the constraints into mathematical expressions.

1. **Budget of Facilities Constraint:**

Exactly $ P $ facilities must be opened.

$$
\sum_{j \in J} y_j = P
$$

Where:

- $ P $ is the number of facilities to open.

2. **Assignment Logic Constraint:**

Customer $ i $ can be served by facility $ j $ only if facility $ j $ is open.

For all $ i \in I $ and $ j \in J $:

$$
x_{ij} \le y_j
$$

3. **Single Service Constraint:**

Each customer is served at most once.

For all $ i \in I $:

$$
\sum_{j \in J} x_{ij} \le 1
$$

4. **Energy/Range Capacity Constraint:**

For each open facility, the sum of round-trip distances to all served customers cannot exceed the maximum energy budget $ R_{max} $.

For all $ j \in J $:

$$
\sum_{i \in I} (2 \cdot d_{ij}) x_{ij} \le R_{max}
$$

Where:

- $ d_{ij} $ is the one-way distance from facility $ j $ to customer $ i $.
- $ R_{max} $ is the maximum total flight range available at a facility.

5. **Mandatory Coverage Constraint:**

Village V1 must be served regardless of which station is opened.

$$
\sum_{j \in J} x_{V1, j} = 1
$$

6. **Binary Constraints:**

The decision variables must be binary.

For all $ i \in I $ and $ j \in J $:

$$
x_{ij} \in \{0, 1\}, \quad y_j \in \{0, 1\}
$$
