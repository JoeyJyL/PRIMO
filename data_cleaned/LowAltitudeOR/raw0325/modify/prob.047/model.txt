Identifying the decision variables, objective function, and constraints for the Risk-Aware UAM Network Design with Overflow Redundancy problem, based on Wei, Gao, Clarke & Topcu (2024), "Risk-aware urban air mobility network design with overflow redundancy", Transportation Research Part B 185, 102967.

### Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

Let's define the decision variables for the problem.

Let's denote:

- $ z_{im} \in \{0,1\} $: binary capacity-selecting variable, equals 1 if backup node $ i $ is assigned capacity $ \bar{c}_{v_i, m} $ (option $ m $), and 0 otherwise. Here $ i = 1, \ldots, \bar{n}_b $ indexes candidate backup nodes and $ m = 1, \ldots, \bar{n}_Z $ indexes capacity options ($ m=1 $ means not built).
- $ X'(ev, k) \geq 0 $: continuous flow matrix for all links and O-D demands under disruption scenario $(ev, k)$, where each entry represents flights assigned to demand $ s_\ell $ traveling through link $ e_j $ per unit time.
- $ D'_1(ev, k) \geq 0 $, $ D'_2(ev, k) \leq 0 $: continuous demand matrices representing fulfilled demand at destinations and departures from origins under scenario $(ev, k)$.
- $ U'_1(ev, k), \ldots, U'_6(ev, k) \geq 0 $: dual variable matrices for the throughput LP under each disruption scenario.
- $ \vec{h}'_1(ev, k), \vec{h}'_2(ev, k) \geq 0 $: dual variable vectors for link and node capacity constraints.
- $ \vec{h}'_3(ev, k) $: dual variable vector for flow balance.
- $ \Lambda_1(ev, k), \Lambda_2(ev, k), \Lambda_3(ev, k) $: auxiliary matrices for linearizing bilinear terms.
- $ \Lambda'_1(ev, k), \Lambda'_2(ev, k), \Lambda'_3(ev, k) $: linearized counterparts satisfying $ \Lambda'_l = \Lambda_l \odot Z $.

Thus, we have:
- $ Z \in \{0,1\}^{\bar{n}_b \times \bar{n}_Z} $: capacity-selecting decision matrix.
- All continuous variables are real-valued and nonnegative where specified.

### Step 2: Objective Function Construction

The goal is to maximize the expected throughput of the extended network under all possible disruption scenarios, minus the weighted construction cost (cost-benefit trade-off).

The expected throughput of the extended network is:

$$
\mathbb{E}[S^*(\mathcal{N}^{ext}(ev,k))] = \sum_{i=1}^{\bar{n}_\mathcal{E}} \sum_{k=1}^{k_{e_i}} S^*(\mathcal{N}^{ext}(e_i, k)) \cdot p_{e_i, k} + \sum_{j=1}^{\bar{n}_\mathcal{V}} \sum_{k=1}^{k_{v_j}} S^*(\mathcal{N}^{ext}(v_j, k)) \cdot p_{v_j, k} + (1 - p_{dis}) S^*(\mathcal{N}(0,0))
$$

The MILP objective function is:

$$
\text{Maximize} \quad (1 - p_{dis}) S^*(\mathcal{N}(0,0)) + \sum_{i=1}^{\bar{n}_\mathcal{E}} \sum_{k=1}^{k_{e_i}} p_{e_i,k} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_S}^T D'_1(e_i,k)^T \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_t} + \sum_{j=1}^{\bar{n}_\mathcal{V}} \sum_{k=1}^{k_{v_j}} p_{v_j,k} \cdot \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_S}^T D'_1(v_j,k)^T \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}} - w \cdot \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_S}^T (Z \odot F) \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_Z}
$$

Where:
- $ w > 0 $ is the cost-benefit valuation parameter.
- $ F $ is the $ \bar{n}_b \times \bar{n}_Z $ cost matrix for all backup nodes.
- $ p_{ev,k} $ is the probability of disruption scenario $ (ev, k) $.
- $ (1 - p_{dis}) S^*(\mathcal{N}(0,0)) $ is the constant contribution from the undisturbed scenario.

### Step 3: Constraint Construction

Now, let's identify and translate the constraints into mathematical expressions.

1. **Throughput LP Feasibility (flow conservation):**

For each disruption scenario $(ev, k)$, the flow matrix $X'(ev,k)$ must satisfy flow conservation:

$$
E'(ev) X'(ev,k) = D'_1(ev,k) + D'_2(ev,k), \quad X'(ev,k) \geq 0
$$

2. **Capacity Constraints (link and node):**

For each disruption scenario $(ev, k)$:

$$
X'(ev,k) \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_S} \leq C^{\mathcal{E}^t}(ev,k), \quad E'(ev)_+ X'(ev,k) \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_S} \leq C^{\mathcal{V}^t}(ev,k)
$$

3. **Demand Matrix Constraints:**

$$
D'_1(ev,k)^T \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}^t} = -D'_2(ev,k)^T \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}^t}
$$
$$
D'_1(ev,k) \leq \Delta'_1(ev) \bar{m}, \quad -D'_2(ev,k) \leq -\Delta'_2(ev) \bar{m}, \quad D'_1(ev,k) \geq 0, \quad D'_2(ev,k) \leq 0
$$

4. **Dual Feasibility (KKT conditions for throughput LP):**

$$
E'(ev)^T U'_1(ev,k) - U'_2(ev,k) + (\vec{h}'_1(ev,k) + E'(ev)_+^T \vec{h}'_2(ev,k)) \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_S}^T = 0
$$

$$
-\mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}^t} \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_S}^T + \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}^t} \vec{h}'^T_3(ev,k) - U'_1(ev,k) + U'_3(ev,k) - U'_5(ev,k) = 0
$$

$$
\mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}^t} \vec{h}'^T_3(ev,k) - U'_1(ev,k) - U'_4(ev,k) + U'_6(ev,k) = 0
$$

$$
U'_2(ev,k), \ldots, U'_6(ev,k) \geq 0, \quad \vec{h}'_1(ev,k), \vec{h}'_2(ev,k) \geq 0
$$

5. **Strong Duality (zero duality gap) for link-disturbed scenarios:**

$$
\mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_S}^T D'_1(e,k)^T \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}^t} = \vec{h}'^T_{11}(e,k) C^\mathcal{E}(e,k) + \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_b}^T \Lambda'_1(e,k) \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_Z} + \vec{h}'^T_{21}(e,k) C^{\mathcal{V}}_0 + \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_b}^T \Lambda'_2(e,k) \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_Z} + \text{tr}(U'^T_3 \Delta'_1 \bar{m}) - \text{tr}(U'^T_4 \Delta'_2 \bar{m})
$$

6. **Strong Duality for node-disturbed scenarios:**

$$
\mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_S}^T D'_1(v,k)^T \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}} = \vec{h}'^T_1(v,k) C^\mathcal{E}_0 + \vec{h}'^T_2(v,k) C^\mathcal{V}(v,k) + \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_b}^T \Lambda'_3(v,k) \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_Z} + \text{tr}(U'^T_3 \Delta'_1 \bar{m}) - \text{tr}(U'^T_4 \Delta'_2 \bar{m})
$$

7. **McCormick Linearization of Bilinear Terms:**

For the backup capacity-dependent terms, using Propositions 2 and 3:

$$
0 \leq \Lambda'_l \leq \bar{m}_\Lambda Z, \quad 0 \leq \Lambda_l - \Lambda'_l \leq \bar{m}_\Lambda (\mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_b} \mathbb{1}^T_{\bar{n}_Z} - Z)
$$

8. **Capacity Selection Constraints:**

Each backup node must be assigned exactly one capacity level:

$$
Z \in \{0,1\}^{\bar{n}_b \times \bar{n}_Z}, \quad Z \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_Z} = \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_b}
$$

9. **Budget Constraint (Tightened):**

Due to reduced government funding, the budget is lowered from 6 to 4:

$$
\mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_S}^T (Z \odot F) \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_Z} \leq f = 4
$$

10. **Mandatory Backup Deployment:**

Government safety regulation requires at least one backup vertiport to be constructed. The "not built" option is prohibited:

$$
z_{i,0} = 0, \quad \forall i = 1, \ldots, \bar{n}_b
$$

Combined with the budget constraint ($f=4$), this forces building the small backup (cost=4, capacity=1) since the large backup (cost=6) exceeds the budget.

### Summary of the Mathematical Model

**Sets:**
- $ \mathcal{V} = \{v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4\} $: Original nodes (vertiports).
- $ \mathcal{E} = \{e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4\} $: Original links (flight corridors).
- $ \mathcal{V}^b = \{v_5\} $: Candidate backup node.
- $ S = \{s_1=(v_1,v_2),\ s_2=(v_1,v_4),\ s_3=(v_3,v_4)\} $: O-D demand pairs.

**Parameters:**
- $ \bar{c}_{v_i} $: Undisturbed node capacity; $ \bar{c}_{e_j} $: Undisturbed link capacity.
- $ p_{ev,k} $: Probability of element $ev$ being disturbed to capacity $c_{ev,k}$.
- $ f = 4 $: Budget (reduced from 6); $ w = 0.1 $: Cost-benefit valuation; $ p_{dis} = 0.8 $.
- $ E $: $ \bar{n}_V \times \bar{n}_E $ incidence matrix; $ E_+ $: element-wise absolute value.
- $ \Delta_1, \Delta_2 $: Destination/origin indicating matrices.
- $ C^{b,all} $: $ \bar{n}_b \times \bar{n}_Z $ potential capacity matrix; $ F $: cost matrix.
- $ \bar{m} $: Sufficiently large constant.

**Decision Variables:**
- $ Z \in \{0,1\}^{\bar{n}_b \times \bar{n}_Z} $: Capacity-selecting matrix.
- $ X'(ev,k), D'_1(ev,k), D'_2(ev,k) \geq 0 $: Flow and demand matrices per scenario.
- $ U'_1 - U'_6, \vec{h}'_1, \vec{h}'_2, \vec{h}'_3, \Lambda_1 - \Lambda_3, \Lambda'_1 - \Lambda'_3 $: Dual and auxiliary variables.

**Objective Function:**

Maximize:

$$
(1 - p_{dis}) S^*(\mathcal{N}(0,0)) + \sum_{e_i \in \mathcal{E}} \sum_{k} p_{e_i,k} \cdot \mathbb{1}^T_{\bar{n}_S} D'_1(e_i,k)^T \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_t} + \sum_{v_j \in \mathcal{V}} \sum_{k} p_{v_j,k} \cdot \mathbb{1}^T_{\bar{n}_S} D'_1(v_j,k)^T \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}} - w \cdot \mathbb{1}^T_{\bar{n}_S} (Z \odot F) \mathbb{1}_{\bar{n}_Z}
$$

**Constraints:** (as listed above, constraints 1-9)
