### Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

Let's define the decision variables for the problem.

Let's denote:

- $ y_j $: a binary variable indicating if vertiport $ j $ is open.
- $ x_{ij} $: a binary variable indicating if zone $ i $ is assigned to vertiport $ j $.
- $ L_j $: a continuous variable representing the total passenger load at vertiport $ j $.

Thus, we have:

- $ y_j \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ j \in J $.
- $ x_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i \in I $ and $ j \in J $.
- $ L_j \ge 0 $ for all $ j \in J $.

### Step 2: Objective Function Construction

The goal is to minimize the total cost, which consists of the fixed costs for opening vertiports, the access costs for passengers, and a quadratic penalty for congestion at the vertiports.

The objective function is:

$$
\min Z = \sum_{j \in J} F_j y_j + \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J} D_i C_{ij} x_{ij} + \sum_{j \in J} \alpha (L_j)^2
$$

Where:

- $ F_j $ is the fixed cost of vertiport $ j $.
- $ D_i $ is the demand of zone $ i $.
- $ C_{ij} $ is the access cost from zone $ i $ to vertiport $ j $.
- $ \alpha = 0.05 $ is the congestion coefficient.

### Step 3: Constraint Construction

Now, let's identify and translate the constraints into mathematical expressions.

1. **Single Assignment Constraint:**

Each zone $ i $ must be assigned to exactly one vertiport.

For each zone $ i \in I $:

$$
\sum_{j \in J} x_{ij} = 1
$$

2. **Vertiport Availability Constraint:**

A zone $ i $ can only be assigned to vertiport $ j $ if vertiport $ j $ is open.

For each zone $ i \in I $ and vertiport $ j \in J $:

$$
x_{ij} \le y_j
$$

3. **Load Definition Constraint:**

The load at vertiport $ j $ is the sum of demand from all zones assigned to it.

For each vertiport $ j \in J $:

$$
L_j = \sum_{i \in I} D_i x_{ij}
$$

4. **Vertiport Capacity Constraint:**

Each vertiport has a maximum capacity $ Q = 200 $ passengers due to limited landing pads.

For each vertiport $ j \in J $:

$$
L_j \le Q \cdot y_j
$$

5. **Binary and Non-negativity Constraints:**

The decision variables must adhere to their respective domains.

$$
x_{ij} \in \{0, 1\}, \quad y_j \in \{0, 1\}, \quad L_j \ge 0
$$
