Identifying the decision variables, objective function, and constraints for the Vertiport Location Planning problem for Urban Air Mobility (UAM), based on Zhao & Feng (2024), "Strategic integration of vertiport planning in multimodal transportation for urban air mobility", Journal of Cleaner Production 467, 142988.

### Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

Let's define the decision variables for the problem.

Let's denote:

- $ y_j $: a binary variable indicating whether candidate vertiport site $ j $ is selected (1) or not (0).
- $ x_{ij} $: a binary variable indicating whether demand point $ i $ is assigned to vertiport $ j $ (1) or not (0).
- $ z_{ij} $: a binary variable indicating whether demand point $ i $ is covered by vertiport $ j $ (1) or not (0), i.e., vertiport $ j $ is selected and within service radius $ R $ of demand point $ i $.

Thus, we have:

- $ y_j \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ j \in V $.
- $ x_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i \in D, j \in V $.
- $ z_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i \in D, j \in V $.

### Step 2: Objective Function Construction

The paper defines three objectives scalarized into a single weighted-sum minimization:

**g1 (Maximize ridership covered):**

$$
g_1 = \sum_{i \in D} \sum_{j \in V} q_i \cdot z_{ij}
$$

**g2 (Minimize total facility cost):**

$$
g_2 = \sum_{j \in V} (c_c + c_o) \cdot y_j
$$

**g3 (Minimize total travel distance from demand points to assigned vertiport):**

$$
g_3 = \sum_{i \in D} \sum_{j \in V} d_{ij} \cdot x_{ij}
$$

The scalarized single objective (converted to minimization) is:

$$
\text{Minimize} \quad -w_1 \cdot g_1 + w_2 \cdot g_2 + w_3 \cdot g_3
$$

Where $ w_1 = 1.0 $, $ w_2 = 0.001 $, $ w_3 = 0.01 $.

### Step 3: Constraint Construction

Now, let's identify and translate the constraints into mathematical expressions.

1. **Hub Proximity Constraint:**

Each selected vertiport must be within the hub distance threshold $ r $ of at least one existing mobility hub. Let $ \mathcal{H}_j = \{h \in H : l_{jh} \leq r\} $ be the set of hubs within threshold $ r $ of site $ j $. If $ \mathcal{H}_j = \emptyset $, site $ j $ is infeasible.

For each $ j \in V $:

$$
y_j \leq |\mathcal{H}_j|
$$

Or equivalently, $ y_j = 0 $ for any $ j $ with $ \mathcal{H}_j = \emptyset $.

2. **Assignment Constraint:**

Each demand point must be assigned to exactly one vertiport.

For each $ i \in D $:

$$
\sum_{j \in V} x_{ij} = 1
$$

3. **Assignment-Selection Linking:**

A demand point can only be assigned to a selected vertiport.

For all $ i \in D $, $ j \in V $:

$$
x_{ij} \leq y_j
$$

4. **Coverage-Selection Linking:**

A demand point can only be covered by a selected vertiport.

For all $ i \in D $, $ j \in V $:

$$
z_{ij} \leq y_j
$$

5. **Coverage Feasibility:**

A demand point $ i $ can only be covered by vertiport $ j $ if their distance does not exceed the service radius $ R $.

For all $ i \in D $, $ j \in V $ such that $ d_{ij} > R $:

$$
z_{ij} = 0
$$

6. **Budget Constraint:**

The total number of selected vertiports cannot exceed $ p $.

$$
\sum_{j \in V} y_j \leq p
$$

### Summary of the Mathematical Model

**Sets:**

- $ D = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\} $: Set of demand points.
- $ V = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\} $: Set of candidate vertiport sites.
- $ H = \{1, 2, 3, 4\} $: Set of existing mobility hubs.

**Parameters:**

- $ q_i $: Ridership (demand) at point $ i \in D $.
- $ d_{ij} $: Euclidean distance from demand point $ i $ to vertiport $ j $ (km).
- $ l_{jh} $: Euclidean distance from vertiport $ j $ to mobility hub $ h $ (km).
- $ R = 3.0 $: Service radius (km).
- $ r = 1.5 $: Hub distance threshold (km).
- $ p = 3 $: Maximum number of vertiports.
- $ c_c + c_o = 500 $: Cost per vertiport (hundred USD).
- $ w_1 = 1.0,\ w_2 = 0.001,\ w_3 = 0.01 $: Objective weights.

**Decision Variables:**

- $ y_j \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ j \in V $.
- $ x_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i \in D,\ j \in V $.
- $ z_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i \in D,\ j \in V $.

**Objective Function:**

Minimize:

$$
-w_1 \sum_{i \in D} \sum_{j \in V} q_i z_{ij} + w_2 \sum_{j \in V} (c_c + c_o) y_j + w_3 \sum_{i \in D} \sum_{j \in V} d_{ij} x_{ij}
$$

**Constraints:**

1. **Hub Proximity:** $ y_j = 0 $ for all $ j \in V $ with $ \mathcal{H}_j = \emptyset $

2. **Assignment:** $ \sum_{j \in V} x_{ij} = 1 $ for all $ i \in D $

3. **Assignment-Selection Linking:** $ x_{ij} \leq y_j $ for all $ i \in D,\ j \in V $

4. **Coverage-Selection Linking:** $ z_{ij} \leq y_j $ for all $ i \in D,\ j \in V $

5. **Coverage Feasibility:** $ z_{ij} = 0 $ for all $ i \in D,\ j \in V $ with $ d_{ij} > R $

6. **Budget:** $ \sum_{j \in V} y_j \leq p $

7. **Variable Domains:** $ y_j, x_{ij}, z_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i \in D,\ j \in V $
