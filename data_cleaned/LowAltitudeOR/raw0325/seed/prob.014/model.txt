### Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

Let’s define the decision variables for the problem.

Let’s denote:

- $ y_i $: a binary variable indicating if Skyport $ i $ is open.
- $ x_{mn} $: a binary variable indicating if demand pair $ (m, n) $ uses Air Taxi (via the nearest open ports).
- $ z_{mn} $: a binary variable indicating if demand pair $ (m, n) $ uses Ground Transport.

Thus, we have:

- $ y_i \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i \in F $.
- $ x_{mn} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ (m, n) \in D $.
- $ z_{mn} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ (m, n) \in D $.

### Step 2: Objective Function Construction

The goal is to minimize the total cost, which consists of the fixed facility costs for Skyports, the total travel time cost for passengers, and the collision risk cost associated with air traffic density.

The objective function is:

$$
\min Z = \sum_{i \in F} c_f y_i + c_t \sum_{(m,n) \in D} (\tau^a_{mn} x_{mn} + \tau^g_{mn} z_{mn}) + C_{risk} \sum_{(i,j)} \sum_{(k,l)} I_{ij,kl} \cdot \text{Flow}_{ij} \cdot \text{Flow}_{kl}
$$

Where:

- $ c_f $ is the fixed facility cost.
- $ c_t $ is the value of time.
- $ \tau^a_{mn} $ is the air travel time for pair $ (m,n) $ via nearest ports.
- $ \tau^g_{mn} $ is the ground travel time for pair $ (m,n) $.
- $ C_{risk} $ is the unit cost of collision risk.
- $ I_{ij,kl} $ is the intersection indicator for air links.
- $ \text{Flow}_{ij} $ is the sum of demand on link $ i \to j $ (derived from $ \sum d_{mn} x_{mn} $ for applicable routes).

### Step 3: Constraint Construction

Now, let’s identify and translate the constraints into mathematical expressions.

1. **Mode Choice Constraint:**

Each demand pair $ (m, n) $ must satisfy their travel requirement by choosing exactly one mode of transport (Air or Ground).

For each demand pair $ (m, n) \in D $:

$$
x_{mn} + z_{mn} = 1
$$

2. **Skyport Availability Constraint:**

The Air Taxi mode can only be selected for demand pair $ (m, n) $ if the nearest candidate skyports for both the origin $ m $ and destination $ n $ are open.

For each demand pair $ (m, n) \in D $:

$$
x_{mn} \le y_{closest(m)}
$$

$$
x_{mn} \le y_{closest(n)}
$$

Where:

- $ closest(m) $ represents the candidate Skyport nearest to origin $ m $.
- $ closest(n) $ represents the candidate Skyport nearest to destination $ n $.

3. **Binary Constraints:**

The decision variables must be binary.

For all $ i \in F $ and $ (m, n) \in D $:

$$
y_i \in \{0, 1\}, \quad x_{mn} \in \{0, 1\}, \quad z_{mn} \in \{0, 1\}
$$

### Summary of the Mathematical Model

The mathematical model for the problem is as follows:

**Decision Variables:**

- $ y_i \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i \in F $.
- $ x_{mn}, z_{mn} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ (m, n) \in D $.

**Objective Function:**

Minimize:

$$
\min Z = \sum_{i \in F} c_f y_i + c_t \sum_{(m,n) \in D} (\tau^a_{mn} x_{mn} + \tau^g_{mn} z_{mn}) + C_{risk} \sum_{(i,j)} \sum_{(k,l)} I_{ij,kl} \cdot \text{Flow}_{ij} \cdot \text{Flow}_{kl}
$$

**Constraints:**

1. **Mode Choice Constraint:**

For each $ (m, n) \in D $:

$$
x_{mn} + z_{mn} = 1
$$

2. **Skyport Availability Constraint:**

For each $ (m, n) \in D $:

$$
x_{mn} \le y_{closest(m)}
$$
$$
x_{mn} \le y_{closest(n)}
$$

3. **Binary Constraints:**

$$
y_i, x_{mn}, z_{mn} \in \{0, 1\}
$$
