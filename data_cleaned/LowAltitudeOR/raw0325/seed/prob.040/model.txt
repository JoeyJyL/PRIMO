Identifying the decision variables, objective function, and constraints for the UAM vertiport network design problem based on the paper "UAM Vertiport Network Design Considering Connectivity" by Zhang and Hwang (2025).

### Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

Let's define the decision variables for the problem.

Let's denote:

- $ X_j $: a binary variable indicating whether a vertiport is constructed at candidate location $ j $ (1) or not (0).
- $ Y_{ij} $: a binary variable indicating whether demand point $ i $ is assigned to vertiport $ j $ (1) or not (0).
- $ l_{jk} $: a continuous variable representing the virtual flow from vertiport $ j $ to vertiport $ k $ (or sink node).

Thus, we have:

- $ X_j \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ j \in J' $.
- $ Y_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i \in I $, $ j \in J' $.
- $ l_{jk} \geq 0 $ for all $ j, k \in J $.

### Step 2: Objective Function Construction

The goal is to minimize the total cost, which includes passenger transportation costs and vertiport construction costs.

The objective function is:

$$
\min \alpha \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J'} h_i d_{ij} Y_{ij} + \sum_{j \in J'} f_j X_j
$$

Where:

- $ \alpha $ is the unit transportation cost per person per kilometer.
- $ h_i $ is the demand (number of passengers) at demand point $ i $.
- $ d_{ij} $ is the Euclidean distance between demand point $ i $ and candidate location $ j $.
- $ f_j $ is the fixed construction cost at candidate location $ j $.

### Step 3: Constraint Construction

Now, let's identify and translate the constraints into mathematical expressions.

1. **Facility Selection Constraint:**

Exactly $ P $ vertiports must be selected from the candidate locations.

$$
\sum_{j \in J'} X_j = P
$$

Where:
- $ P $ is the number of vertiports to be constructed.

2. **Demand Assignment Constraint:**

Each demand point must be assigned to exactly one selected vertiport.

For each demand point $ i \in I $:

$$
\sum_{j \in J'} Y_{ij} = 1
$$

3. **Assignment Feasibility Constraint:**

A demand point can only be assigned to a vertiport that has been selected.

For each demand point $ i \in I $ and candidate location $ j \in J' $:

$$
Y_{ij} - X_j \leq 0
$$

4. **Flow Balance Constraint:**

For each selected vertiport (except the virtual sink node), the flow conservation must be maintained to ensure connectivity.

For each candidate location $ j \in J' $:

$$
X_j + \sum_{n \in N_j} l_{nj} = \sum_{m \in N_j} l_{jm}
$$

Where:
- $ N_j $ is the set of candidate locations within maximum flight distance $ D_{max} $ from location $ j $.

5. **Non-negativity Constraint:**

Virtual flows must be non-negative.

For all $ j, k \in J $:

$$
l_{jk} \geq 0
$$

6. **Flow Capacity Constraint:**

The outgoing flow from each vertiport is limited by whether it is selected.

For all $ j, k \in J' $:

$$
l_{jk} \leq (P - 1) X_j
$$

7. **Sink Node Inflow Constraint:**

No flow enters the virtual sink node from other nodes.

For all $ j \in J' $:

$$
l_{1j} = 0
$$

Where node 1 represents the virtual sink node.

8. **Sink Node Outflow Constraint:**

The flow from any vertiport to the sink node is either 0 or $ P $ (total number of facilities).

For all $ j \in J' $:

$$
l_{j1} \in \{0, P\}
$$

9. **Sink Node Selection Constraint:**

The virtual sink node is not selected as an actual vertiport location.

$$
X_1 = 0
$$

10. **Node Set Definition:**

The complete node set $ J $ includes candidate locations $ J' $ and the virtual sink node.

$$
J' \cup \{1\} = J
$$

11. **Neighborhood Feasibility Constraint:**

The neighborhood set $ N_j $ contains all candidate locations within the maximum flight distance.

For each candidate location $ j \in J' $:

$$
N_j = \{n \in J' \mid d_{jn} \leq D_{max}\}
$$

Where:
- $ D_{max} $ is the maximum allowable flight distance between vertiports.

The mathematical model for the UAM vertiport network design problem is as follows:
