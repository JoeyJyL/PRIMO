### Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

Let’s define the decision variables for the problem.

Let’s denote:

- $ y_d $: a binary variable indicating if depot $ d $ is opened.
- $ x_{ij} $: a binary variable indicating if a drone travels from node $ i $ to node $ j $.
- $ t_i $: a continuous variable representing the arrival time (latency) at node $ i $.

Thus, we have:

- $ y_d \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ d \in D $.
- $ x_{ij} \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i, j \in N $.
- $ t_i \ge 0 $ for all $ i \in N $.

### Step 2: Objective Function Construction

The goal is to minimize the total cost, which consists of the fixed costs for opening depots and the total latency experienced by the customers.

The objective function is:

$$
\min Z = \sum_{d \in D} F_d y_d + \sum_{i \in C} t_i
$$

Where:

- $ F_d $ is the fixed cost of opening depot $ d $.
- $ t_i $ is the arrival time at customer node $ i $.
- $ D $ is the set of candidate depots and $ C $ is the set of customers.

### Step 3: Constraint Construction

Now, let’s identify and translate the constraints into mathematical expressions.

1. **Flow Conservation Constraints:**

Each customer must be visited exactly once (standard VRP constraints).

For each customer $ j \in C $:

$$
\sum_{i \in N} x_{ij} = 1
$$

For each customer $ i \in C $:

$$
\sum_{j \in N} x_{ij} = 1
$$

2. **Depot Association Constraints:**

Routes must start at opened depots. If a depot is not opened, no flow can leave it.

For each depot $ d \in D $:

$$
\sum_{j \in N} x_{dj} \le M y_d
$$

3. **Time Propagation (Cumulative Latency):**

If a drone travels from $ i $ to $ j $, the arrival time at $ j $ is the arrival time at $ i $ plus the travel time. This also eliminates subtours.

For all valid arcs $ (i,j) $:

$$
t_j \ge t_i + \tau_{ij} - M(1 - x_{ij})
$$

The arrival time at the depots is initialized to 0.

For each depot $ d \in D $:

$$
t_d = 0
$$

4. **Binary and Non-negativity Constraints:**

The decision variables must adhere to their respective domains.

$$
y_d \in \{0, 1\}, \quad x_{ij} \in \{0, 1\}, \quad t_i \ge 0
$$

### Summary of the Mathematical Model

The mathematical model for the problem is as follows:
