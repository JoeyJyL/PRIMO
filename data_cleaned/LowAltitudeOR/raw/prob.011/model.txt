### Step 1: Decision Variables Definition

Let’s define the decision variables for the problem.

Let’s denote:

- $ y_i $: a binary variable indicating if vertiport $ i $ is opened.
- $ z_k $: a binary variable indicating if OD route $ k $ (pair $ (i, j) $) is active.

Thus, we have:

- $ y_i \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ i \in N $.
- $ z_k \in \{0, 1\} $ for all $ k \in K $.

### Step 2: Objective Function Construction

The goal is to maximize the robust net profit, which is the revenue from active routes (considering demand uncertainty) minus the fixed costs of opening vertiports.

The objective function is:

$$
\text{Maximize } Z = \sum_{k \in K} z_k \cdot (D_k^{nom} - \hat{D}_k) \cdot P_k^{air} \cdot Margin_k^{air} - \sum_{i \in N} F_i y_i
$$

Where:

- $ D_k^{nom} $ is the nominal daily demand for route $ k $.
- $ \hat{D}_k $ is the demand uncertainty margin for route $ k $.
- $ P_k^{air} $ is the probability of choosing Air Taxi for route $ k $.
- $ Margin_k^{air} $ is the net revenue per air passenger for route $ k $.
- $ F_i $ is the fixed cost of opening vertiport $ i $.
- $ N $ is the set of candidate vertiports and $ K $ is the set of potential OD routes.

### Step 3: Constraint Construction

Now, let’s identify and translate the constraints into mathematical expressions.

1. **Route Activation Logic:**

A route $ k = (i, j) $ can be active only if both its origin vertiport $ i $ and destination vertiport $ j $ are opened.

For each route $ k = (i, j) \in K $:

$$
z_k \le y_i
$$

$$
z_k \le y_j
$$

2. **Binary Constraints:**

The decision variables must be binary.

For all $ i \in N $ and $ k \in K $:

$$
y_i \in \{0, 1\}, \quad z_k \in \{0, 1\}
$$

### Summary of the Mathematical Model

The mathematical model for the problem is as follows:

**Decision Variables:**

1. **Route Activation Logic:**
